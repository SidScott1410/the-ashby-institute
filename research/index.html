RESEARCH PROGRAMS

Four Programs.
One Law.

Every research program at TAI is grounded in the same structural premise: Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety. A regulator that cannot model its system cannot govern it. We apply this constraint to compute, governance, society, and the full range of complex systems that define the modern world.

CROSS-DOMAIN APPLICATIONS

One Law Governs Every System

AI Alignment

V(AI) ≫ V(H)

Superintelligent systems exceed human regulatory variety; the alignment problem is a variety-matching failure.

Cybersecurity

V(attacker) > V(defender)

Persistent insecurity arises when attackers hold more variety than defenders. Defense requires modeling the full attack surface.

Autonomous Systems

V(env) ≤ V(controller)

Autonomous vehicles, drones, and robotic systems fail when environmental variety exceeds controller model fidelity.

Critical Infrastructure

V(grid) ≤ V(operator)

Power grid failures and supply chain collapse follow from insufficient operator variety relative to system complexity.

Financial Systems

V(market) > V(regulator)

Systemic financial risk accumulates when market complexity exceeds regulatory model capacity (2008 as a GRT failure).

Healthcare Systems

V(pathogen) > V(response)

Pandemic response failures are variety failures: public health systems that cannot model novel pathogens cannot control them.

Democratic Governance

V(polity) > V(institution)

Democratic institutions that cannot model the full variety of their polity lose legitimacy and effectiveness.

Climate & Earth Systems

V(climate) > V(policy)

Climate governance fails when policy instruments lack the variety to absorb the complexity of Earth system feedbacks.
