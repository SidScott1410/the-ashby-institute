THEORETICAL FOUNDATION

The Theory

W. Ross Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety (1956) and the Good Regulator Theorem (Conant & Ashby, 1970) are not metaphors. They are mathematical results that discipline AI economics: neither markets nor institutions can be understood without modeling the systems and constraints that shape them.

REGULATORY NETWORK · FEEDBACK DYNAMICS
CELLULAR AUTOMATON · EMERGENT VARIETY

ASHBY · 1956

Law of Requisite Variety

V(R) ≥ V(D)

VARIETY OF REGULATOR ≥ VARIETY OF DISTURBANCE

Only variety can absorb variety. A regulator can reduce the variety of outcomes in a system only to the extent that it possesses at least as much variety as the disturbances it must absorb.

This is not a design principle; it is a mathematical constraint. It applies to thermostats, immune systems, financial regulators, AI oversight architectures, and the institutions that shape an AI economy alike.

"Only variety can destroy variety." — W. Ross Ashby, 1956

CONANT & ASHBY · 1970

The Good Regulator Theorem

∀S ∃R: R ≡ model(S)

EVERY GOOD REGULATOR MUST BE A MODEL OF ITS SYSTEM

The GRT is a stronger result than Ashby's Law. It proves that any regulator achieving good regulation must, necessarily, contain an internal model of the regulated system. Model-building is not optional; it is the mechanism of regulation itself.

A regulatory body that lacks an adequate internal model of the system it governs cannot regulate it effectively, not because of insufficient effort or political will, but because of a structural impossibility.

THE ULTIMATE APPLICATION

AI Alignment as a Variety Problem

V(AI) ≫ V(H)

AI VARIETY VASTLY EXCEEDS HUMAN REGULATORY VARIETY

As AI systems grow in capability, their variety (the range of states they can occupy and outputs they can produce) will exceed the variety of any human oversight system. This is not a prediction about malevolence. It is a mathematical constraint on the possibility of control.

The GRT adds a second constraint: any system that successfully aligns an AI must model that AI. As AI systems become more capable, the model required for alignment becomes more complex, potentially as complex as the AI itself.

APPLICATIONS

The Law Applied Across Domains

AI Alignment

V(AI) ≫ V(H)

A superintelligent AI operates with variety vastly exceeding human regulatory capacity. Human oversight hits a mathematical ceiling, not a political one.

Compute Governance

V(compute) > V(regulator)

AI-native compute systems generate variety faster than regulatory institutions can model. Governance deficits are structurally inevitable without deliberate design.

Financial Systems

V(market) > V(regulator)

The 2008 crisis was a GRT failure: regulators lacked the internal model complexity to anticipate the variety of structured credit instruments.

Cybersecurity

V(attacker) > V(defender)

Persistent insecurity is a variety problem. Defenders who cannot model the full attack surface cannot defend it.

Democratic Governance

V(polity) > V(institution)

Democratic institutions that cannot model the full variety of their polity lose legitimacy and effectiveness over time.

Critical Infrastructure

V(system) > V(operator)

Cascading infrastructure failures follow from operator models too simple to anticipate the variety of failure modes in complex coupled systems.
